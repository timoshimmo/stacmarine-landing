01.

Dominating Offshore Production: FPSOs and FSOs

FPSOs (Floating Production, Storage and Offloading) and FSOs (Floating Storage and Offloading) have become the workhorses of offshore oil and gas production in many regions worldwide. These massive ship-shaped facilities play a crucial role by serving as centralized hubs for extracting, processing, storing, and offloading oil and gas reserves.
02.

Adaptable and Enduring

FPSOs and FSOs are designed for versatility. Secured to the seabed with mooring systems specifically chosen for the prevailing environmental conditions (water depth, currents, etc.), they can operate in a wide range of locations, from relatively shallow continental shelves to deep ocean environments.  These robust facilities are engineered to withstand harsh conditions and can remain on station for extended periods, often exceeding 20 years, ensuring long-term productivity.
03.

FPSOs: Processing Powerhouses

An FPSO goes beyond simple storage. It's a self-contained production unit. Fluids, including crude oil, water, and associated gas, are extracted from subsea reservoirs through risers, which are essentially large pipes connecting the FPSO to the seabed. Once onboard, the FPSO's processing facilities separate the extracted fluids into usable components. Crude oil is then stored in onboard tanks until it's ready for offloading. Separated natural gas can be either re-injected into the reservoir for pressure maintenance or processed and exported. Water and impurities are treated and disposed of responsibly.
04.

Streamlined Offloading

When the storage tanks of an FPSO reach capacity, the crude oil is transferred to shuttle tankers, smaller vessels specifically designed for efficient oil transport. These shuttle tankers then carry the oil to refineries or directly to market. This streamlined offloading process ensures minimal disruption to FPSO operations, allowing for continuous production.
PROCESS BREAKDOWN

How we use FPSO to extract energy resources

step 1

Energy Extraction

Imagine a giant straw reaching down to the seabed (subsea wells). Through this straw, a mix of things come up: oil, gas, and water (hydrocarbons). This mixture is like unrefined oil you might see before it's processed.
step 2

Processing Plant

Once onboard the FPSO, this mixture goes to a separation plant. Think of it like a high-tech recycling center for oil, gas, and water. Oil: The usable oil gets stored in giant tanks on the FPSO (crude oil).

Gas

The natural gas can either be:

Piped back to land for further processing.
Put back into the reservoir to help push more oil out (like adding water to a ketchup bottle).

Water

The saltwater that comes up with the oil is treated and either:

Used for various things onboard the FPSO.
Pumped back into the reservoir (depending on regulations).
step 3

Storage Tank

The separated oil is like the finished product waiting for delivery. It's stored in giant tanks onboard the FPSO until it's ready to be shipped.
step 4

Delivery

When it's time to ship the oil, special tanker ships called "shuttle tankers" come alongside the FPSO.

The oil gets pumped from the FPSO's storage tanks onto the shuttle tankers, which take it to refineries or markets. An FPSO acts like a processing center and storage unit at sea, all in one massive vessel!
